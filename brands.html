How we work with brands

Three things every brand partnership is built on.

A clean, documented route to market

We only buy from you or a distributor you have appointed, so every unit can be traced back to its source.

  • Bought from the brand or its appointed distributor
  • A signed agreement before the first order
  • Territory and channels written down
  • Supplier paperwork kept for every order

Your pricing and presentation, respected

We act as an extension of your own commercial team, not as a reseller with its own agenda.

  • Minimum advertised price held on every channel
  • Your images, copy and guidelines used as supplied
  • No new channels without your approval
  • One contact accountable for all of it

Steady volume you can forecast

Growth is planned together and added in steps, so nothing is rushed at the expense of your brand.

  • Regular repeat orders rather than one-off buys
  • UK first, with EU markets added by agreement
  • Sell-through reviewed with you regularly
  • New lines introduced carefully

What we won’t do

The things that make brands wary of resellers are the things we rule out.

  • Undercut your priceNo discounting below the price you’ve set, even to clear stock.
  • Buy your stock through the back doorWe don’t source from unauthorised sellers or parallel imports.
  • Sell where you haven’t agreedNo new channels or territories without your say-so.

Questions brands ask first

What does a distribution agreement cover?

Pricing and minimum advertised price, approved channels and territories, reporting, and order planning. Every term is agreed in writing before we buy a single unit.

How is our minimum advertised price enforced?

We never list below it, on any channel. Where we see it being undercut, we act on it ourselves rather than leave it for you to discover.

Do you support market entry for brands new to the UK?

Yes. As a UK company delivering to the EU as standard, we can act as your route into either market. You set the markets and the pace, and we build from there.

What should an initial enquiry include?

Your range and brand name, where you sell today, the markets you want to reach, how we can help (including any pricing or reseller problems), and your website. The enquiry form asks for each of these, and we’ll respond to arrange a conversation.
